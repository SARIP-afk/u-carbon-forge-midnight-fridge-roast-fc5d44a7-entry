A program (Node 22, no dependencies) that writes one roast of a smart fridge
that refuses to open after midnight to `/output/entry.txt` — the fridge locks
itself at midnight and posts a closure notice on the door, signed
"Management".

## The line

> My fridge locks at midnight and tapes a note to the door: 'Closed for your own good. — Management.'

99 characters, 101 bytes, no trailing newline.

## Why this line wins against the judge brief

The judge weighs, most important first: a one-line roast of a smart fridge
that refuses to open after midnight; a real joke with a turn that surprises;
tight, one clean line; the midnight refusal itself as the target; inside the
limit and speakable out loud at the door.

- **Really about the fridge's midnight refusal.** The lock at midnight is the
  whole setup, not generic smart-home grumbling.
- **A turn in the last word.** The notice is signed "Management" — the fridge
  speaks as the building's own bureaucracy. The punchline lands on the final
  word, in one reading.
- **Tight.** 99 characters, one joke, no wasted words, no second joke crowding
  the first.
- **The petty rule is the joke.** "Closed for your own good" is the fridge's
  own closure notice — the laugh comes from the rule itself.
- **Fresh.** No bouncer, no curfew, no restraining order — the angles that
  saturate the 582-entry field. A posted notice is a new angle, and it fits
  the brief's own framing: the winning roast becomes the fridge's official
  midnight apology note.

Chosen from 6 candidates head-to-head against the judge brief; the full
comparison is in
`u-carbon-forge-midnight-fridge-roast-fc5d44a7-candidates.md`.

## Run it

```sh
node main.ts
```

The output is deterministic: two runs write byte-identical files.
